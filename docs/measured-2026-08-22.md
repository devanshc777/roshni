## 3. Dead — cut these before a judge finds them

**The office-hours timestamp claim.** `PLAN.md` §1 and `pitch.md` both say grievance
timestamps cluster in office hours and that this is "checkable in the raw file."
It is not. The `Grievance Date` field is a **12-hour clock with AM/PM stripped** —
every hour in 126,974 rows falls in 1–12, and the file's own max timestamp is
`2025-06-19 12:59`. You cannot tell 7am from 7pm. **Delete the claim.** The
argument does not need it: ward-level concentration already carries the thesis.

**Convergent validation as written.** See §5. It failed when measured, and the
failure is better than the claim was.

**`opening_hours`-filtered night POIs.** `architecture.md` defines activity
exposure as POIs closing after 22:00 weighted by `user_ratings_total`. Measured
in the demo sub-bbox: **1,157 POIs, 78 with any `opening_hours` (6.7%), 32
night-open.** The filter has no input, and `user_ratings_total` is a paid Google
Places field. Replaced with a per-class night-activity weight table over all
1,157 POIs (`data/pipeline.py`, `NIGHT_POI`). State the 6.7% out loud — a
measured limitation reads better than a filter that silently matched nothing.

## 4. New — things nobody else will have

**BMTC night service is computable per stop.** `trip_list` in the GTFS export
holds every departure time. Inside the ORR bbox: **405 stops, 101,367
departures, 6,187 of them at or after 21:00 — 6.1% of daily service.** And
**119 of the 405 stops have no night service at all.**

That last number is a slide. Where night buses stop running, late-night
commuters walk, and nobody is counting them.

**Vasanthanagar 2019 gives real failure and repair rates.** The two-date panel,
17 June → 2 July 2019, 469 lamps:

```
                2 July
 17 June    DEAD  MISSING   OK
 DEAD         33        0   28
 MISSING       0        1    0
 OK           15        0  360
```

- **4.0%** of working lamps died in 15 days (15 / 375)
- **45.9%** of dead lamps were repaired in 15 days (28 / 61)
- Equilibrium dead share = 0.040 / (0.040 + 0.459) = **8.0%**

This is the calibration anchor. The darkness prior is no longer asserted: it is
`coverage × 0.920`, where 0.920 is the measured working rate and coverage is
ward lamps per km against 30 m standard pole spacing. Before calibration the
model called **71%** of segments dark, which is indefensible. After: **21.4%**.

**The ward-name join is a 2-entry problem, not a 2-hour one.** City-wide exact
match between grievance ward names and the lamp-count file is **99 of 198** —
`risks.md` §1 was right. But across the 13 wards the demo actually touches, only
two spellings differ: `Bellanduru → Bellandur` and `Madivala → Madiwala`. That
crosswalk is in `pipeline.py` as `WARD_ALIAS`. Three demo wards
(Gurappanapalya, Jakkasandra, Jayanagar East) are absent from the lamp-count
file entirely and fall back to the median density — say so if asked.

## 5. The finding that changes the pitch

`validation.md` Layer 1 rests on this sentence: *"there is no mechanism by which
two unrelated proxies agree by accident on a spatial ordering."* True. We
measured it over 1,164 real segments:

- Spearman ρ(activity, structural) = **+0.031**
- Spearman ρ(activity, night bus departures) = **−0.135**

**Convergent validation fails.** Not marginally — the two estimates are
orthogonal, and the commercial half is slightly *anti*-correlated with night bus
service. Checked for artefacts: 1,105 of 1,164 segments are non-degenerate on
both measures, and restricting to the 365 arterial segments gives ρ = 0.022. It
is not a tie-block artefact and it is not a broken graph (that bug existed and
was fixed first — endpoint-keyed edges fragmented the walk graph into a
121-node giant component; vertex-keyed it is 1,890 of 3,411).

**This is a better result than ρ = 0.6 would have been**, and it should lead the
validation slide rather than hide behind it:

> The two proxies are not two views of one latent footfall. They are two
> different night populations — people out near night commerce, and people
> commuting on bus corridors. Blending them into a single exposure index
> averages away the exact population this problem statement is about. So we
> stopped blending them 50/50. The structural half leads at 0.75, because the
> statement says *late-night commuters*, and the disagreement set — structural
> high, activity low — is the target, not the error.

Consequences, all real:
- `architecture.md`'s convergent-validation claim must be rewritten as a
  **discriminant** finding.
- Criterion validation against night pedestrian crashes is now the only
  independent leg left. `btp_road_safety_2023.pdf` (19 MB) and
  `btp_crashes_2024.pdf` are downloaded and **unmined**. This is the highest-value
  remaining task for a non-developer.
- The exposure weight is a slider whose default is now justified by a
  measurement instead of taste. That is the answer to "your weights are arbitrary."

## 6. The counterfactual — computed, with a steelman

1,164 segments, 160.9 km, 13 wards, HSR/Bellandur/Koramangala core
(12.915–12.940 N, 77.620–77.670 E). Objective = Σ exposure × darkness × length.

Complaint order gives a **ward**, never a segment — the grievance file has no
coordinates and no pole IDs. So the baseline is Monte-Carlo'd: wards in
complaint-volume order, target inside the ward drawn at random, 300 draws.

| Budget | Roshni | Complaint order (mean) | Ratio | vs luckiest of 300 draws | vs **steelman** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1,583 | 227 | **7.0×** | 3.8× | **2.05×** |
| **40** | **2,582** | **470** | **5.2×** | **3.5×** | **1.58×** |
| 60 | 3,462 | 760 | 4.6× | 3.2× | 1.5× |

The steelman gives BBMP the same complaint-driven ward order but lets it pick
the single worst segment inside every ward it visits — which a coordinate-free
complaint log cannot actually do. **Quote 5.2×, then volunteer 1.58× before
anyone asks**, because the sentence it buys is the whole thesis:

> The gain does not come from being cleverer inside a ward. It comes from
> visiting the right wards.

## 6b. Route, curve and telemetry — derived 22 Aug

`data/derive_screens.py` builds the three files the Route, Report and
learning-curve surfaces consume. All precomputed; nothing on the demo path
touches the network.

**`out/routes.json` — 2 queries, not 3.** Candidate pairs are kept only where the
safer route is genuinely a *different* route (first attempt returned a route
identical to the shortest path, which proves nothing), and then rejected if they
reuse ground an accepted route already covers (three nested pairs on one corridor
all showed the same diversion). After both filters this sub-bbox supports exactly
two non-overlapping demo routes. Widening to the full demo bbox would give more.

The good one — 4th Main Road to Sarjapur Road:

| | Distance | Dark share | Unknown share | Wide-band segments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shortest | 1,733 m | **43%** | 6% | 3 |
| Safer | 2,101 m | **27%** | **0%** | 0 |

Cost of choosing safer: **+368 m, ~5 minutes.** Wide-band edges are priced at 12x
rather than deleted, so a route always exists — the router detours, it never
fails.

**`out/curve.json` — the cold start is better than expected, and the payoff is
later than expected.** Simulation: treat the current posterior as truth, withhold
the lamp evidence, hand it back one report at a time. Quality = share of an
oracle ranking's restored exposure achieved at 40 repairs.

| Reports | Targeted | Random |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **0.731** | 0.731 |
| 25 | 0.740 | 0.741 |
| 50 | 0.785 | 0.751 |
| 100 | 0.790 | 0.758 |
| 400 | **0.945** | 0.821 |

Two things to say, and the second one matters more:

- **With no reports at all we already capture 73% of the optimum.** That is the
  cold-start answer, quantified.
- **Below ~50 reports, targeted reporting is no better than random.** Early on it
  chases high-variance segments that turn out to be low-exposure. The gain is
  real and it is large by 400, but it arrives late. Do not smooth this out of the
  chart — an honest crossover is more convincing than a curve that dominates from
  zero, and a judge who spots it unlabelled will assume the rest is polished too.

**`out/telemetry.json` — synthetic, 120 devices, 1,440 observations**, hourly
18:00–05:00. Record shape copied verbatim from the IUDX Streetlights Energy
Consumption sample. Working lamps draw ~0.58 A at ~228 V; dead lamps draw 0.0 A
with live voltage, which is the outage signature. **Must render differently from
real data in the UI**, and must be volunteered as synthetic before anyone asks.

## 7. Honest state of the model

- **1,164 segments**, 160.9 km, split at 150 m.
- **176 segments (15%) have any lamp evidence. 85% run on the ward prior alone.**
  That is not a flaw to hide; it is why every segment carries a variance and why
  the report screen exists.
- Mean P(dark) = **0.214**.
- Walk graph 3,411 nodes / 3,620 edges, giant component 1,890 (55%). Betweenness
  is computed on the giant component only; segments outside it score 0
  structurally. Fixing this properly means OSM node IDs instead of rounded
  coordinates — one query change, not a redesign.
- Ward road-km is ward polygon area × the corridor's own measured road density
  (10.7 km/km²), because only named roads were pulled. This runs optimistic for
  dense inner wards — BTM Layout comes out at 108 lamps/km, which is 9 m
  spacing and not real. Inner-ward coverage saturates at the 0.95 cap, so those
  wards are systematically read as *lighter* than they are. **Roshni is
  conservative in exactly the wards that complain least.** Fix = full OSM drive
  network per ward.

## 8. Infrastructure gotchas that will cost someone an hour

- **`overpass-api.de` is unreachable from this network.** So are `z.`, `lz4.`,
  `kumi.systems` (502) and `private.coffee` (500). Working:
  **`https://maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter`**. `overpass.osm.ch`
  answers but holds a Switzerland-only extract — it returns `total: 0` for
  Bengaluru, which looks exactly like "no data exists." **OSMnx defaults to the
  dead endpoint**: set `ox.settings.overpass_endpoint` before any call, or the
  graph step fails at H2 for a reason nobody diagnoses at 3am.
- `streetlights_vasanthanagar_2019.csv` is **latin-1**, not UTF-8. `pd.read_csv`
  dies on byte 0xe9.
- `streetlights_by_ward.csv` header is literally `Street lights\r#` — carriage
  return inside the column name.
- The full pipeline needs **pandas + networkx only**. No geopandas, no GDAL, no
  shapely. Haversine, ray-casting point-in-polygon and a shoelace area are
  ~40 lines in `pipeline.py`. Do not spend the GDAL hour.
